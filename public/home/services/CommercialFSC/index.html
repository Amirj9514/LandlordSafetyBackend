Commercial Fire Alarm Assessment (CFSC)

Commercial fire alarm systems are tested and certified to BS 5839-1, the British Standard covering fire detection and alarm systems in non-domestic premises — a different standard to the Grade A–D system used for residential lettings. Systems are classified into Category M (manual call points only) or Category L1–L5 (automatic detection, with L1 providing the most comprehensive coverage and L5 tailored to a specific fire safety objective).

Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, the "responsible person" for a commercial premises must ensure fire detection is suitable and sufficient for the risk — and while the law doesn't mandate a specific system, BS 5839-1 compliance is the accepted way to demonstrate that duty. Systems must be tested and serviced regularly by a competent person, with routine weekly checks and periodic professional servicing.

Requirements You Should Know

The fire safety rules landlords need to stay on top of — clearly explained.

  1. 01

    System Categories

    Category M is manual call points only; Category L1–L5 covers automatic detection, with L1 the most comprehensive and L4/L5 more limited to escape routes or specific risks.

  2. 02

    Legal Basis

    The Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 requires the responsible person to ensure fire detection is suitable for the risk — BS 5839-1 is the accepted standard for demonstrating this.

  3. 03

    Testing Frequency

    Weekly checks by the responsible person, plus periodic professional servicing and testing — typically every 6 months, based on the fire risk assessment.

  4. 04

    Who Determines the Category

    The required category is usually identified through a Fire Risk Assessment, based on the premises' layout and risk level.

  5. 05

    Duration of Your Appointment

    Typically 1–3 hours depending on system size and category; larger multi-zone systems take longer.

What's the difference between Category M, L and P systems?

Under BS 5839-1, Category M systems rely on manual call points only, with no automatic detection. Category L systems use automatic detection to protect life: L1 covers the whole building; L2 covers escape routes, rooms opening onto them and defined higher-risk areas; L3 covers escape routes and rooms opening onto them; L4 covers escape routes only; and L5 is designed around a specific fire risk. Category P systems protect property, with P1 covering the whole building and P2 covering defined areas.

Which category does my premises need?

This is determined by your Fire Risk Assessment, based on the building's layout, use, occupancy and risk level. There's no fixed rule for all commercial premises.

How often does the system need testing?

BS 5839-1 recommends a weekly test by the responsible person, operating a different manual call point each time, plus a professional inspection and service roughly every 6 months. Your fire risk assessment, insurer or lease may require more frequent servicing.

Is a commercial fire alarm certificate a legal requirement?

Indirectly. The Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 requires appropriate fire detection and warning based on risk, kept in good working order. Designing, installing and servicing to BS 5839-1 is the recognised way to show compliance.

What happens if a fault is found during testing?

We'll record any faults or non-compliant devices in the logbook and on your service report, and provide a clear breakdown and quote for the work needed to restore compliance.
