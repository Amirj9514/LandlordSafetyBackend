Domestic Electric Certificate (EICR)

An Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) is a legal requirement for every private rental property in England. Under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020, landlords must have the fixed electrical installation inspected and tested at intervals of no more than five years by a qualified person, and obtain a report of the results.

The inspector checks the consumer unit, earthing and bonding, and tests circuits throughout the property, issuing observation codes where needed: C1 (danger present — immediate action), C2 (potentially dangerous — urgent remedial action), and C3 (improvement recommended, non-mandatory). You must provide a copy to new tenants before they move in, to existing tenants within 28 days of the inspection, and to your local authority within 7 days if requested. Landlords without a valid EICR face civil penalties of up to £30,000 per breach, and an invalid or missing EICR can block a Section 21 or Section 8 possession claim.

Requirements You Should Know

Our certified Gsc assessments are affordable and tailored for residential properties:

  1. 01

    The 5-Year Renewal Window

    Your EICR is valid for a maximum of five years from the date of issue, provided the report is satisfactory and no earlier reinspection is recommended. Older properties with ageing wiring are sometimes given a shorter interval by the inspecting electrician.

  2. 02

    Serving the Report to Tenants

    New tenants must receive a copy before they move in. Existing tenants must receive a copy within 28 days of the inspection. Your local authority must receive a copy within 7 days if they request one.

  3. 03

    Remedial Work Timeframes

    Any C1 (danger present) or C2 (potentially dangerous) observation must be remediated within 28 days, or sooner if the report specifies. C3 items are recommended but not mandatory, F1 Indicates further Investigation is required.

  4. 04

    Non-Compliance Penalties

    Local authorities can issue civil penalties of up to £30,000 per breach. A missing or expired EICR can also block a valid Section 21 or Section 8 possession notice..

  5. 05

    Duration of Your Appointment

    Typically 3–5 hours for an average 3-bedroom home, depending on the number of circuits and consumer units. Larger properties, HMOs or multi-unit blocks take longer.

How often do I need a Gas Safety Certificate?

Every 12 months, without exception. You can carry out the check up to 2 months before the current certificate expires without losing your renewal date.

What happens if I don’t have a valid CP12?

It’s a criminal offence, not just an administrative one — it can lead to unlimited fines and, in serious cases, prosecution. A missing or expired certificate also invalidates a Section 21 notice, so you can’t regain possession of the property without a valid record in place.

What does the inspection actually check?

Every gas appliance, pipe, fitting and flue you provide as landlord — boilers, hobs, gas fires, cookers — checked for leaks, safe combustion, and correct ventilation.

Do I need to give tenants a copy?

Yes. A copy must go to tenants within 28 days of the check, and to new tenants before they move in. You’re required to keep records for at least 2 years.

What if an appliance fails the inspection?

The appliance will be marked as “At Risk” or “Immediately Dangerous” on the certificate and must not be used until repaired by a Gas Safe registered engineer. Your engineer will explain the fault and what remedial work is needed. We can re-inspect once repairs are complete so you can obtain a compliant CP12.
