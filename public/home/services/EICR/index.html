Domestic Electrical Certificate (EICR)

An Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) is a legal requirement for every private rental property in England. Under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020, landlords must have the fixed electrical installation inspected and tested at intervals of no more than five years by a qualified person, and obtain a report of the results.

The inspector checks the consumer unit, earthing and bonding, and tests circuits throughout the property, issuing observation codes where needed: C1 (danger present — immediate action), C2 (potentially dangerous — urgent remedial action), and C3 (improvement recommended, non-mandatory). You must provide a copy to new tenants before they move in, to existing tenants within 28 days of the inspection, and to your local authority within 7 days if requested.

Landlords without a valid EICR face civil penalties of up to £30,000 per breach, and an invalid or missing EICR can block a Section 8 possession claim.

Requirements You Should Know

What every landlord needs to know about EICR.

  1. 01

    The 5-Year Renewal Window

    Your EICR is valid for a maximum of five years from the date of issue, provided the report is satisfactory and no earlier reinspection is recommended. Older properties with ageing wiring are sometimes given a shorter interval by the inspecting electrician.

  2. 02

    Serving the Report to Tenants

    New tenants must receive a copy before they move in. Existing tenants must receive a copy within 28 days of the inspection. Your local authority must receive a copy within 7 days if they request one.

  3. 03

    Remedial Work Timeframes

    Any C1 (danger present), C2 (potentially dangerous) or FI (further investigation required) item must be dealt with within 28 days, or sooner if the report specifies. C3 items are recommended but not mandatory. Landlords must give written confirmation of completed work to tenants and the local authority within 28 days.

  4. 04

    Non-Compliance Penalties

    Local authorities can issue civil penalties of up to £30,000 per breach.

  5. 05

    Duration of Your Appointment

    Typically 3–5 hours for an average 3-bedroom home, depending on the number of circuits and consumer units. Larger properties, HMOs or multi-unit blocks take longer.

How often is an EICR required for rental properties?

At least every 5 years, or sooner if the previous report specifies a shorter interval. It’s a legal requirement under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020.

What does BS 7671 mean?

It’s the UK standard for electrical wiring and installations that an EICR is assessed against.

What happens if my property fails the inspection?

Observations are coded C1 (danger present), C2 (potentially dangerous), C3 (improvement recommended) or FI (further investigation). Any C1, C2 or FI must be fixed or investigated within 28 days, or sooner if the report says so. You must then give written confirmation of the completed work to tenants and the local authority within 28 days. Councils can issue civil penalties of up to £30,000 for breaches.

Do I need to give tenants a copy of the EICR?

Yes. Existing tenants must receive a copy within 28 days of the inspection, and new tenants before they move in. The local authority must receive a copy within 7 days if it asks for one.

What’s checked during an EICR?

The condition of the fuse board, wiring, sockets, switches, light fittings, and earthing and bonding throughout the property, checked for damage, wear and compliance with current safety standards.

How long does an EICR inspection take?

Typically 1–3 hours for a standard residential property, depending on size and the number of circuits.

Do you cover EICR inspections outside London?

We cover all 32 London boroughs and within M25 London.
