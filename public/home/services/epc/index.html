Energy Performance Certificate (EPC)

Since 1 April 2020, landlords in England and Wales must ensure their rental properties have a valid Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) with a minimum rating of E before granting a new tenancy or renewing an existing one. Failing to comply can result in fines of £500 to £5,000.

An EPC rates your property from A (most efficient) to G (least efficient). Under MEES regulations, properties rated F or G cannot legally be rented unless a valid exemption applies.

Requirements You Should Know

Our certified EPC assessments are affordable and tailored for residential properties:

  1. 01

    Failing an EPC

    Selling a property has no minimum rating to meet — just a valid EPC in place. Letting a property requires a minimum E rating; below that, you can’t move new tenants in.

  2. 02

    MEES (Minimum Energy Efficiency Standards)

    Since April 2018, all privately rented properties in England and Wales must hold a minimum EPC rating of E — extended to existing residential tenancies in April 2020 and commercial lettings in April 2023.

  3. 03

    MEES Exemptions

    A property can fall outside MEES in a handful of situations: certain listed buildings, holiday lets and some HMOs that don’t legally require an EPC; tenancies under 6 months or over 99 years; cases where every cost-effective improvement has already been made; a confirmed devaluation of more than 5%; consent for works refused by a third party; and social housing or licence-only occupiers, which sit outside MEES entirely.

  4. 04

    Your Legal Obligations

    An EPC has been required for selling, letting or constructing a building since 2008. Estate agents must include it in all advertising; landlords are responsible for keeping it valid.

  5. 05

    Duration of the Appointment

    Around 20 minutes for a studio flat, up to an hour for a larger home. Non-domestic surveys can take considerably longer.

How quickly will I receive my certificate after the inspection?

Every certificate is issued within 24 hours of the inspection — usually the same evening. You receive it by email as an official PDF, ready to share with tenants or agents.

Are your engineers Gas Safe registered?

Yes. Every gas engineer we send is Gas Safe registered. Electrical work is carried out by NICEIC affiliated electricians.

How often does a landlord need a Gas Safety Certificate?

Landlords must have gas appliances and flues checked every 12 months. A new CP12 is required before a new tenancy starts if the current certificate has expired.

How often is an EICR required for rental properties?

Residential rental properties in England need an EICR at least every 5 years, or at the start of a new tenancy. HMOs and commercial properties may have different requirements.

Do I need an EPC for my rental property?

Yes. A valid EPC (minimum rating E) must be provided to tenants before marketing a property and is required for every new tenancy.

What happens if my property fails an inspection?

Your engineer will explain any issues found and advise on remedial work. We can often return to re-inspect once faults are fixed so you can obtain a compliant certificate.

Do you cover my area?

Can I book multiple certificates for the same property?

Absolutely. You can book gas, electrical, fire, and EPC certificates in one visit where possible — saving time and reducing disruption for you and your tenants.
