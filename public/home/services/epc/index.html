Energy Performance Certificate (EPC)

An Energy Performance Certificate, or EPC, is a legal requirement for any property being sold or rented in the UK. Without a valid EPC, you cannot legally market your property — and you risk fines of up to £500 for residential and £5,000 for commercial properties.

An EPC rates your property's energy efficiency on a scale from A (most efficient) to G (least efficient), and shows your estimated annual energy costs. Rental properties must achieve a minimum rating of E under current MEES legislation — or you cannot let them out.

Requirements You Should Know

Our certified EPC assessments are affordable and tailored for residential properties:

  1. 01

    Failing an EPC

    Selling a property has no minimum rating to meet — just a valid EPC in place. Letting a property requires a minimum E rating; below that, you can’t move new tenants in.

  2. 02

    MEES (Minimum Energy Efficiency Standards)

    Since April 2018, all privately rented properties in England and Wales must hold a minimum EPC rating of E — extended to existing residential tenancies in April 2020 and commercial lettings in April 2023.

  3. 03

    MEES Exemptions

    A property can fall outside MEES in a handful of situations: certain listed buildings, holiday lets and some HMOs that don’t legally require an EPC; tenancies under 6 months or over 99 years; cases where every cost-effective improvement has already been made; a confirmed devaluation of more than 5%; consent for works refused by a third party; and social housing or licence-only occupiers, which sit outside MEES entirely.

  4. 04

    Your Legal Obligations

    An EPC has been required for selling, letting or constructing a building since 2008. Estate agents must include it in all advertising; landlords are responsible for keeping it valid.

  5. 05

    Duration of the Appointment

    Around 20 minutes for a studio flat, up to an hour for a larger home. Non-domestic surveys can take considerably longer.

Is an EPC a legal requirement?

Yes. A property in England and Wales needs a valid EPC when it's built, sold or let, and it must be provided to prospective buyers or tenants. For rentals, the minimum rating rules apply to existing tenancies as well as new ones.

What's the minimum EPC rating for a rental property?

Under current MEES rules, rental properties must be rated E or above, unless a valid exemption is registered. The government has confirmed plans to raise this to EPC C for all private tenancies by 1 October 2030, with legislation to follow.

How long does an EPC last?

10 years from the date of issue. You can check whether a property already has a valid certificate on the government's EPC register.

How long does the assessment take?

Usually under an hour for a standard home, depending on size and layout. Larger homes and HMOs may take longer.

Can my property fail an EPC?

There's no pass or fail for a sale. For lettings, a property rated F or G can't legally be let to new or existing tenants unless an exemption is registered.

What happens if I don't have a valid EPC?

Failing to provide an EPC when letting or selling a home can lead to a £200 penalty from Trading Standards. Letting a property below the minimum rating without an exemption can lead to civil penalties of up to £5,000.

Are there any exemptions to the minimum E rating?

Yes. The main exemptions cover cases where all relevant improvements have been made up to the spending cap, where third-party consent is refused, where works would reduce the property's value by more than 5%, and a temporary 6-month exemption for new landlords. Exemptions must be registered on the PRS Exemptions Register.
