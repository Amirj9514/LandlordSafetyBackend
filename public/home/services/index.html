What certificates do landlords legally need for a rental property?

At minimum: a Gas Safety Certificate if the property has gas appliances, an EICR, a valid EPC rated E or above, and working smoke and carbon monoxide alarms. HMOs and buildings with shared common areas also need a Fire Risk Assessment and, in most cases, a fire alarm system that meets licensing requirements.

Can I book multiple services in one visit?

Yes. Many landlords combine gas, electrical, fire and EPC inspections into a single visit to save time and reduce disruption to tenants.

Do all these services apply to every property, or only some?

It depends on the property. Gas certificates only apply where gas appliances are present. PAT testing isn't a legal requirement in England but is recommended for landlord-supplied appliances, and is often a condition of HMO licences. Fire Risk Assessments and fire alarm certification mainly apply to HMOs and shared buildings.

How often does each certificate need renewing?

Gas Safety Certificates every 12 months, EICRs at least every 5 years, and EPCs every 10 years. Panel-based fire alarm systems (Grade A) should be serviced at least every 6 months, while interlinked alarms should be tested regularly. Fire Risk Assessments should be reviewed regularly and after any significant change.

What happens if I let a property without the required certificates?

It varies by certificate. Missing gas or electrical safety records can lead to prosecution or civil penalties of up to £30,000. Letting below the minimum EPC rating can lead to fines, and fire safety failings can lead to enforcement action or refusal of an HMO licence.

Do you offer installation as well as certification?

Yes. We install boilers, fuse boards, fire alarm systems, smoke and CO alarms, and fire safety equipment, alongside our certification and testing services.

Are your engineers accredited for every service you offer?

Yes. Gas work is carried out by Gas Safe registered engineers and electrical work by NICEIC or NAPIT registered electricians, with qualified assessors for EPCs, fire risk assessments and asbestos surveys.

Do you cover HMOs as well as single-let properties?

Yes. We work with private landlords, HMO landlords, and letting agents managing portfolios of any size.

What certificates does a commercial property legally need?

It depends on the premises, but most need: a Fire Risk Assessment, gas appliances maintained in a safe condition (with annual inspection as the accepted standard), electrical systems maintained safely (usually shown through an EICR), and a valid EPC when the building is sold, let or built. Buildings completed before 2000 also fall under the duty to manage asbestos. Fire alarms and emergency lighting are required where your Fire Risk Assessment identifies them.

How is commercial gas certification different from residential?

Residential lettings use a single CP12 record. Commercial premises use different records depending on the equipment: CP17 for the gas installation, CP15 for commissioning and servicing commercial appliances, CP42 for catering kitchens, and CP44 for mobile catering units.

How often does a commercial EICR need renewing?

It depends on the premises. Under IET Guidance Note 3, offices, shops, restaurants and hotels are typically inspected every 5 years, industrial units and leisure complexes every 3 years, and swimming pools, petrol stations and launderettes every year. Your report will specify the exact interval.

Do commercial properties need an EPC?

Yes, when a building is sold, let or built, with limited exemptions such as places of worship and temporary buildings. Failing to provide one can lead to a penalty of 12.5% of the building's rateable value, between £500 and £5,000. Letting a building rated F or G without a registered exemption can lead to much larger fines.

Is a Fire Risk Assessment a legal requirement for my business?

Yes. Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, the responsible person must carry out and regularly review a Fire Risk Assessment. Since October 2023, it must be recorded in writing in full, regardless of the size of the business.

Do you work with multi-tenant commercial buildings?

Yes. We work with landlords, letting agents and facilities managers, from single retail units to multi-floor commercial buildings.

Can you handle all our compliance certificates in one visit?

In most cases, yes. Gas, electrical, fire and PAT inspections can often be scheduled together to minimise disruption to your business.

Do you cover commercial premises outside London?

We cover all 32 London boroughs and within M25 London.

Do I need Building Regulations approval for a new fuse board?

In homes, replacing a fuse board (consumer unit) is notifiable work under Part P of the Building Regulations. The work is carried out by NICEIC or NAPIT registered electricians who self-certify it, and you'll receive a Building Regulations compliance certificate once it's notified. Commercial installations aren't covered by Part P but are carried out to BS 7671.

What's included with a new boiler installation?

Full installation by a Gas Safe registered engineer, the manufacturer's Benchmark commissioning checklist to support your warranty, and a Building Regulations Compliance Certificate issued through Gas Safe Register.

Can you install a fire alarm system to match what my HMO licence requires?

Yes. We'll install to the grade and category your Fire Risk Assessment or council specifies, whether that's a Grade D1 interlinked system for a smaller property or a Grade A system with a central panel for a larger HMO.

Do all rental properties need smoke and CO alarms?

Yes. Landlords must fit a smoke alarm on every storey used as living accommodation, and a carbon monoxide alarm in any room with a fixed combustion appliance, such as a gas boiler, gas fire or wood burner. Gas cookers are excluded.

What happens if your engineer finds a fault during diagnostics?

You'll get a clear, itemised breakdown of the fault and the remedial work needed, along with a quote, before any further work goes ahead.

Do you only install, or can you fix existing systems too?

Both. We handle new installations as well as fault finding and repair on existing boilers, electrics and fire systems.

Is emergency lighting a legal requirement for my building?

It depends on the building. Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, commercial premises and the common areas of larger HMOs and blocks of flats need emergency lighting where the Fire Risk Assessment identifies it.
