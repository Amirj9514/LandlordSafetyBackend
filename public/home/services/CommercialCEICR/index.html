Commercial Electrical Assessment (CEICR)

An Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) is a formal inspection of a commercial premises' fixed wiring, consumer units, sockets and circuits, assessed against BS7671. Under the Electricity at Work Regulations 1989, employers and duty holders are legally required to keep electrical systems safe — an EICR is the standard, widely accepted way to demonstrate that duty is being met. The Health and Safety Executive (HSE) can prosecute where unsafe electrical installations cause injury, with penalties including unlimited fines and, in serious cases, imprisonment.

Unlike residential lettings, commercial premises don't have a single fixed renewal period — the recommended interval depends on the type and risk level of the premises: typically every 5 years for offices and retail, but every 1–3 years for higher-risk environments such as industrial units, commercial kitchens, swimming pools, and public buildings.

Requirements You Should Know

The fire safety rules landlords need to stay on top of — clearly explained.

  1. 01

    Legal Basis

    The Electricity at Work Regulations 1989 place a duty on employers and duty holders to keep electrical systems safe; an EICR is the standard way to demonstrate compliance.

  2. 02

    Renewal Interval Varies by Risk

    Offices and retail: typically every 5 years. Industrial units, kitchens, and public buildings: typically every 1–3 years, based on a risk assessment.

  3. 03

    Insurance Requirement

    Most commercial insurers require a valid, in-date EICR as a condition of cover — a lapsed certificate can affect a claim following an electrical incident.

  4. 04

    INon-Compliance Penalties

    HSE enforcement can include unlimited fines and, in serious cases, imprisonment where unsafe electrics cause injury.

  5. 05

    Duration of Your Appointment

    Typically 1–5 hours depending on premises size and number of circuits; larger sites may need multiple visits.

How often do I need a Gas Safety Certificate?

Every 12 months, without exception. You can carry out the check up to 2 months before the current certificate expires without losing your renewal date.

What happens if I don’t have a valid CP12?

It’s a criminal offence, not just an administrative one — it can lead to unlimited fines and, in serious cases, prosecution. A missing or expired certificate also invalidates a Section 21 notice, so you can’t regain possession of the property without a valid record in place.

What does the inspection actually check?

Every gas appliance, pipe, fitting and flue you provide as landlord — boilers, hobs, gas fires, cookers — checked for leaks, safe combustion, and correct ventilation.

Do I need to give tenants a copy?

Yes. A copy must go to tenants within 28 days of the check, and to new tenants before they move in. You’re required to keep records for at least 2 years.

What if an appliance fails the inspection?

The appliance will be marked as “At Risk” or “Immediately Dangerous” on the certificate and must not be used until repaired by a Gas Safe registered engineer. Your engineer will explain the fault and what remedial work is needed. We can re-inspect once repairs are complete so you can obtain a compliant CP12.
