Requirements You Should Know

What you need to know about electrical work.

  1. 01

    Who Can Carry It Out

    Notifiable work, such as new circuits or rewiring, must be self-certified by a registered electrician (NICEIC, NAPIT or similar) or signed off by Building Control.

  2. 02

    Part P Notification

    New circuits or rewiring in a dwelling are notifiable under Part P — self-certified through our scheme or notified to Building Control.

  3. 03

    Certification Provided

    You'll receive an Electrical Installation Certificate (EIC) for new work, or a Minor Works Certificate for smaller repairs and alterations.

  4. 04

    Common Fault Indicators

    If you get a shock from an appliance or socket, or smell burning, switch off at the board and stop using it. We can usually arrange a next-day visit to find and fix the fault. If there's smoke or fire, call 999.

  5. 05

    Duration of Your Appointment

    Typically 1–3 hours for a diagnostic visit or minor repair; rewiring or larger installation work may take a full day or more depending on scope.

How do I know if I need a diagnostic visit or a repair?

If you're experiencing tripping breakers, flickering lights or unexplained power loss, a diagnostic visit finds the cause first. If parts are available and the fix is straightforward, we can often complete the repair in the same appointment.

Do I need to notify Building Control for electrical work?

In homes, new circuits, rewires, consumer unit replacements and certain work in bathrooms and shower rooms are notifiable under Part P of the Building Regulations. Our NICEIC or NAPIT registered electricians self-certify this work, and you'll receive a Building Regulations compliance certificate. Minor repairs and like-for-like replacements aren't notifiable. Part P doesn't apply to commercial premises, but all work is carried out to BS 7671.

What should I do while waiting for an engineer?

If you notice burning smells, scorch marks, buzzing, or sockets that feel hot, switch off the affected circuit or the main switch at your fuse board if it's safe to do so, and don't keep resetting a tripping breaker. If there's any sign of fire, leave the property and call 999.

Will I get a certificate for the work?

Yes. New installations and alterations are issued with an Electrical Installation Certificate (EIC) or a Minor Electrical Installation Works Certificate, depending on the scope of the work.
