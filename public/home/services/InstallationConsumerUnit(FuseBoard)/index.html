Consumer Unit Installation (Fuse board)

Your consumer unit (fuse board) is the control centre of your property's electrics — every circuit in the building runs through it, and it's what protects you from fire or electric shock when something goes wrong. Older boards with rewireable fuses, plastic housings or no RCD protection often fall short of current safety standards and are commonly flagged on an EICR. Whether you're upgrading an outdated board, replacing a damaged one, or fitting a new installation from scratch, the work must be carried out to the current edition of BS7671 (the IET Wiring Regulations) by a qualified electrician.
Consumer unit replacement is notifiable work under Part P of the Building Regulations — it must either be self-certified by a competent person scheme member (such as NICEIC or NAPIT) or notified to and signed off by Building Control. Either route leaves you with a Building Regulations compliance certificate and an Electrical Installation Certificate (EIC) for your records.

RCD (Residual Current Device)

Monitors electrical current and instantly cuts power if it detects a leak to earth — protects against electric shock.

RCBO (Residual Current Breaker with Overcurrent protection)

Combines RCD shock protection and standard circuit breaker overload protection in one device per individual circuit — so a fault on one circuit doesn't trip the whole board.

SPD (Surge Protection Device)

Protects your consumer unit and connected appliances from voltage spikes — caused by lightning strikes or switching surges on the network.

Distribution Board (Commercial)

The commercial equivalent of a consumer unit — houses the circuit breakers and protective devices for a commercial property's electrical supply, often single-phase or three-phase depending on the site's power demand.

Requirements You Should Know

What you need to know before replacing a consumer unit.

  1. 01

    Part P Notification

    Consumer unit replacement must be self-certified by a registered electrician or notified to Building Control — unnotified work is a legal breach and can complicate a future property sale.

  2. 02

    Current Wiring Regulations

    All new boards must comply with the current edition of BS7671, including appropriate RCD/RCBO protection for relevant circuits.

  3. 03

    Who Can Carry It Out

    Only a qualified, registered electrician (NICEIC, NAPIT or similar) can legally self-certify this work under a competent person scheme.

  4. 04

    Existing Faults

    A new board with RCD protection can expose hidden faults on existing circuits. If found, we'll explain them and quote for any remedial work before going live.

  5. 05

    Duration of Your Appointment

    Typically 4–6 hours for a standard residential board replacement; commercial distribution boards may take longer depending on site and circuit count.

Do I need permission to replace my fuse board?

Not permission as such. In homes, replacing a consumer unit is notifiable work under Part P of the Building Regulations. It must either be carried out by an electrician registered with a competent person scheme, such as NICEIC or NAPIT, who self-certifies the work, or be notified to Building Control before work starts. Commercial installations aren't covered by Part P but must still meet BS 7671.

How long does a fuse board replacement take?

Typically half a day for a standard residential property, during which the power will be off. It can take longer if testing finds existing faults on any circuits that need to be fixed before the new board can be safely energised.

Will I get a certificate afterwards?

Yes. You'll receive an Electrical Installation Certificate (EIC) on completion. For domestic work, you'll also receive a Building Regulations compliance certificate, issued through the competent person scheme once the work has been notified.

Can you upgrade my fuse board and carry out my EICR at the same time?

Yes. Many clients combine the two, especially when a fuse board replacement is recommended as part of EICR remedial work.

Do you install consumer units in commercial premises?

Yes. We install and replace distribution boards for offices, retail units and other commercial premises, designed to suit the circuits and load required.
