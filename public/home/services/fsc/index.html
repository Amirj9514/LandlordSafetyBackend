Fire Alarm Certificate (FSC)

A Fire Alarm Certificate confirms that your property's fire detection and alarm system has been tested, serviced, and meets the relevant British Standard. Most rental properties need at least a Grade D system with interconnected alarms, while HMO’s typically require a more comprehensive Grade A system with a central control panel — the exact grade and category depends on your property's layout and size.

Fire alarm systems must be inspected and serviced by a competent person at intervals not exceeding six months. For HMO’s specifically, this is a licensing requirement under the Housing Act 2004 — without a valid, in-date certificate, your HMO license can be refused or revoked.

Requirements You Should Know

The fire safety rules landlords need to stay on top of — clearly explained.

  1. 01

    Testing Frequency

    Fire alarm systems must be inspected and serviced by a competent person at intervals not exceeding six months.

  2. 02

    System Grade

    Most rental properties need a minimum Grade D system with interconnected alarms; HMOs typically require Grade A with a central control panel, depending on layout and storeys.

  3. 03

    HMO Licensing

    For HMOs, a valid fire alarm certificate is required to obtain or renew your HMO licence under the Housing Act 2004. Local authorities can refuse or revoke a licence without one.

  4. 04

    Who Can Certify

    Testing and certification must be carried out by a competent person — typically an accredited fire alarm engineer.

  5. 05

    Record Keeping

    Certificates and logbooks should be retained and made available on request to your local authority or fire safety inspector.

How often does my fire alarm system need testing?

At intervals not exceeding six months, carried out by a competent person.

Do I need a Grade A or Grade D system?

It depends on your property type and fire risk assessment. Most standard rental homes use Grade D — mains-powered, interlinked detectors. HMOs and larger or higher-risk properties often require Grade A, with a central control panel, call points and detectors. Your local council’s HMO licence conditions will confirm the grade required.

Is a fire alarm certificate required for HMO licensing?

Yes. Licensed HMOs must have a suitable fire detection system maintained to BS 5839-6. Councils typically require an inspection and servicing certificate, kept on file and produced within 7 days if requested. Without a valid, in-date certificate, your HMO licence can be refused or revoked.

What happens if a fault is found during testing?

Any faults are recorded on the certificate and reported to you. Defective detectors, sounders, batteries or panel equipment must be repaired or replaced before the system is considered fully compliant. We can return to re-test once remedial work is complete.

Who is allowed to test and certify a fire alarm system?

Periodic inspection and servicing must be carried out by a competent person with specialist knowledge of fire detection and alarm systems. For Grade A systems this is normally a qualified fire alarm engineer. Routine weekly or monthly tests on simpler Grade D systems can be done by the occupier, but certification should always be issued by a competent specialist.

How long is the certificate valid for?

The certificate confirms your system was compliant at the time of inspection. Under BS 5839-6, Grade A systems must be inspected and serviced at least every six months, so you should renew on that schedule. HMO landlords may also need to submit updated certificates to their local council every six months.
