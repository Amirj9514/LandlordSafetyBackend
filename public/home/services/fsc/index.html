Fire Alarm Certificate (FSC)

A Fire Alarm Certificate confirms that your property's fire detection and alarm system has been tested, serviced, and meets the relevant British Standard. Most rental properties need at least a Grade D system with interconnected alarms, while HMO’s typically require a more comprehensive Grade A system with a central control panel — the exact grade and category depends on your property's layout and size.

Fire alarm systems must be inspected and serviced by a competent person at intervals not exceeding six months. For HMO’s specifically, this is a licensing requirement under the Housing Act 2004 — without a valid, in-date certificate, your HMO license can be refused or revoked.

Requirements You Should Know

The fire safety rules landlords need to stay on top of — clearly explained.

  1. 01

    Testing Frequency

    Fire alarm systems must be inspected and serviced by a competent person at intervals not exceeding six months.

  2. 02

    System Grade

    Most rental properties need a minimum Grade D system with interconnected alarms; HMOs typically require Grade A with a central control panel, depending on layout and storeys.

  3. 03

    HMO Licensing

    For HMOs, a valid fire alarm certificate is required to obtain or renew your HMO licence under the Housing Act 2004. Local authorities can refuse or revoke a licence without one.

  4. 04

    Who Can Certify

    Testing and certification must be carried out by a competent person — typically an accredited fire alarm engineer.

  5. 05

    Record Keeping

    Certificates and logbooks should be retained and made available on request to your local authority or fire safety inspector.

How often does my fire alarm system need testing?

It depends on the system grade. Panel-based Grade A systems should be inspected and serviced by a competent person at least every 6 months under BS 5839-6. Interlinked Grade D alarms should be tested regularly, at least monthly, and replaced in line with the manufacturer's guidance.

Do I need a Grade A or Grade D system?

It depends on your property and your local council's requirements. Most single lets and smaller HMOs need Grade D1 interlinked alarms. Larger HMOs, typically those with three or more storeys, usually need a Grade A system with a central control panel.

Is a fire alarm certificate required for HMO licensing?

In most cases, yes. Councils typically ask for a current fire alarm certificate with HMO licence applications and renewals, and a missing certificate can delay or prevent approval.

What happens if a fault is found during testing?

We'll record any faults or non-compliant detectors and provide a clear breakdown and quote for the work needed to bring the system back into compliance.

Who's allowed to test and certify a fire alarm system?

A competent person with the relevant training and experience in fire detection and alarm systems.

How long is the certificate valid for?

A certificate confirms the system was working at the time of the visit. For Grade A systems, a new servicing certificate is issued at each 6-monthly service. Check your HMO licence for any specific renewal requirements.

Do you cover fire alarm testing outside London?

We cover all 32 London boroughs and within M25 London.
