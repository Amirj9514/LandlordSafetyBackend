Commercial Gas Safety Assessment (CP42)

Commercial gas safety certification covers a broader scope than a residential CP12, reflecting the higher output and wider range of gas appliances found in hotels, restaurants, offices and other commercial premises. Inspections are split across four certificate types: CP15 for boiler commissioning and servicing, CP17 for the full gas installation, CP42 for commercial kitchens, and CP44 for mobile catering units.

Under the Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998, any gas appliance, pipework or flue on commercial premises must be maintained in a safe condition, with regular checks carried out by a Gas Safe registered engineer. Non-compliance can result in enforcement action, unlimited fines, and — where negligence causes harm — prosecution of the responsible person.

What’s Included

Four commercial gas certificate types, matched to your premises and equipment — here’s who needs which.

CP15 — Boiler Commissioning & Servicing

For any premises with a commercial boiler — offices, hotels, retail units. Confirms the boiler is commissioned or serviced correctly and performing safely, with a full service record issued.

CP17 — Gas Installation Safety Report

For any commercial premises with gas appliances, pipework or flues. Covers the full gas installation, not just the boiler, and is the standard certificate most businesses need for general compliance.

CP42 — Catering Gas Safety Certificate

For fixed commercial kitchens — restaurants, cafes, school and hotel kitchens. Covers fryers, ovens, grills and other catering gas appliances, tested by an engineer qualified specifically in commercial catering gas.

CP44 — Mobile Catering Gas Safety Certificate

For food trucks, market stalls and mobile catering units running on LPG. Covers the same safety checks as CP42, adapted for mobile and bottled-gas setups.

Requirements You Should Know

What businesses need to know about commercial gas safety.

  1. 01

    Which Certificate You Need

    Kitchens and restaurants need a CP42, food trucks a CP44, and premises with a boiler a CP15. A CP17 covers the full gas installation, and most premises need a combination.

  2. 02

    Annual Renewal

    All four certificates should be renewed every 12 months. Most councils, insurers and landlords expect an in-date certificate, so we'll remind you before yours is due.

  3. 03

    Engineer Competency

    Each certificate requires a Gas Safe engineer qualified in that specific area — a domestic gas engineer can't legally issue a CP17, CP42 or CP44.

  4. 04

    Insurance Requirement

    Most commercial insurers require a valid, in-date certificate matching your equipment type to maintain cover.

  5. 05

    Duration of Your Appointment

    Typically 1–3 hours depending on certificate type and number of appliances; larger kitchens take longer.

What’s the difference between CP15, CP17, CP42 and CP44?

These are the industry record forms used by Gas Safe registered engineers. CP17 is the gas installation safety report for commercial premises, covering pipework, plant, flues and ventilation. CP15 records the commissioning and servicing of commercial gas appliances such as boilers. CP42 covers commercial catering kitchens and appliances, and CP44 covers mobile catering units. Which ones you need depends on the gas equipment on site.

How often does commercial gas certification need renewing?

The Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998 require gas appliances and installations in commercial premises to be maintained in a safe condition. Annual inspection is the accepted industry standard and is expected by most insurers and, for food businesses, by environmental health officers.

Do all commercial premises need a CP42?

No. A CP42 is only needed where there are commercial catering gas appliances. An office with a gas boiler would typically need a CP17, plus a CP15 when the boiler is serviced.

What happens if an appliance fails inspection?

The engineer flags it on site. If it’s an immediate danger, it will be isolated with your permission. The defect is recorded on the report, and you’ll receive a clear quote for remedial work. Once the work is done, the appliance is retested and certified.
