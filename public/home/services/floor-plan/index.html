Professional Floor Plan

A professional floor plan gives an accurate, to-scale layout of your property — room dimensions, doors, windows and overall footprint — used for property listings, EPC assessments, and compliance or planning records. Unlike a rough sketch, a surveyed floor plan is measured on site, so the dimensions shown are accurate rather than estimated.

Floor plans aren't a legal requirement on their own, but they're commonly needed alongside an EPC assessment, when marketing a property for sale or let, or when submitting planning or building control applications. Many letting agents and portals now expect a floor plan as standard alongside listing photos.

What's included in a floor plan?

A measured floor plan of the property showing the layout, room names, room dimensions and total floor area, supplied digitally and ready to use in listings or applications.

Do I need a floor plan to let or sell a property?

It isn't a legal requirement, but floor plans are widely used in property listings and are often requested by letting agents and buyers.

Can a floor plan be used for an HMO licence application?

Yes. Councils usually ask for a floor plan with HMO licence applications, showing room sizes and uses. Since 2018, bedrooms in licensed HMOs must meet minimum sizes: 6.51 sq m for one adult, 10.22 sq m for two adults, and 4.64 sq m for a child under 10. Tell us what your council's application requires when you book.

Can I book a floor plan with my EPC?

Yes. Many landlords book both in the same visit, since the property is measured for both.

How accurate are the measurements?

Rooms are measured on site, and plans show approximate dimensions suitable for listings and licensing. They aren't intended as architectural or building survey drawings.

Do you cover floor plans outside London?

We cover all 32 London boroughs and within M25 London.
